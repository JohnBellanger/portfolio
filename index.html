1. Context: Business Problem & Scope

Core Business Question:

"Why did escalating advertising expenditures on Meta Ads fail to generate proportional gross revenue growth, and which specific acquisition segments, regional markets, and attribution distortions are destroying capital?"

Making media allocation decisions based solely on platform self-reported metrics exposes leadership to unknowingly subsidizing unprofitable customer acquisition. To eliminate guesswork, this audit joins raw operational tables from Meta Ads Manager with verified first-party transactions in Google Analytics 4 using BigQuery. The objective is to identify margin leaks, isolate the structural causes of negative ROAS in December, and reallocate capital toward provably profitable segments.

2. Methodology & Decision-Making Visual Standards (Protocols)

Data Cleaning & Pipeline Protocol: Raw ad-click and transaction logs routinely contain duplicate tracking hits, mismatched currency conversions, and attribution overlaps. I duplicated raw tables in BigQuery to maintain immutable data lineage, standardized timestamp and date formats across logs, cleaned strings using LOWER() and TRIM() for naming consistency, converted expense columns to unified numeric data types, and joined tables into a normalized flat table for reliable cross-platform validation.

Standard Distribution Protocol: Every statistical metric across descriptive and diagnostic sections is consistently evaluated across four dimensions: Percentages (relative budget consumed vs revenue generated), Minimum & Maximum (absolute efficiency boundaries vs worst-case deficits), and Mean vs. Median (evaluating arithmetic averages against median baselines to identify distribution skewness and volatility spikes via the +15% rule).

3. Phase 1: Macro Performance Audit (ROAS vs. CPA Trajectory)

Explanatory Cap & Business Problem: Before auditing individual ad sets, executive leadership must establish the aggregate trajectory of channel efficiency to identify the precise inflection point where unit economics broke down.

The Why & The How: We hypothesized that customer acquisition costs outpaced conversion values toward year-end, driving aggregate ROAS below the break-even threshold. ROAS evaluates gross capital productivity (Revenue / Spend), while CPA measures direct customer acquisition expense (Spend / Orders).

Applied Calculation: ROAS = Attributed Revenue / Total Ad Spend | CPA = Total Ad Spend / Verified Conversions

Distributions & Empirical Findings: ROAS hit a floor in December (falling below the critical 1.0 break-even line) versus a peak efficiency of 7.19 during properly timed seasonal promotions. The aggregate Q4 average was heavily distorted by pre-holiday weeks, masking rapid late-season margin erosion.

Conditional Decision Rules (IF / ELSE):

IF ROAS < 1.0 (Break-Even Threshold): The channel burns cash on marginal sales → Action: Immediate scaling freeze and auction bid audit. (December period isolated in Orange on chart)

ELSE: The channel generates positive gross contribution margin → Maintain or scale budgets predictably. (Compliant months rendered in Gray)
